    What the numbers can tell you.

    A model grid, not a street

    NASA POWER provides MERRA-2 reanalysis, which combines observations with a model. The native grid is 0.5° latitude by 0.625° longitude. Local conditions can differ, especially in complex terrain.

    Whole years, fair comparisons

    Temperature uses a day-weighted annual mean. Precipitation adds monthly rates multiplied by the days in each month. A year is excluded if any month is missing.

    An estimate with limits

    A straight line estimates change per decade. Adjusted intervals, a robust slope, and fixed date checks show sensitivity. They use the same data and cannot independently prove the result.

    Read the statistical method and limitations

    The annual chart uses ordinary least squares. Its band describes the fitted mean under independent, normally distributed, constant-variance errors. The ordinary slope interval uses Student t with n − 2 degrees of freedom. Its p-value tests a zero slope under these assumptions. The chart is not a forecast.

    The comparison uses statsmodels Newey-West covariance with a Bartlett kernel, n/(n−2) correction, and t(n−2) critical values. The automatic lag is floor(4(n/100)^(2/9)); fixed lags 1 and 5 expose tuning sensitivity. We withhold these intervals if a calendar year is missing. Short-record coverage can be poor, as our synthetic evaluation shows.

    Theil-Sen is the median of pairwise slopes, shown as an outlier sensitivity check without a dependence-adjusted confidence interval. Removing each year once checks single-year influence. Fixed date checks trim five calendar years at each end, retaining at least ten complete years. Their range is descriptive, not a confidence interval.

    These methods do not address dataset bias, all forms of temporal dependence, structural breaks, or multiple comparisons. Repeatedly trying locations and periods can produce apparently significant results by chance. The lag-one warning threshold of 0.3 is only a heuristic. We make no causal or drought-risk claim.

    Rates are rounded at source, so precipitation totals are approximate.
