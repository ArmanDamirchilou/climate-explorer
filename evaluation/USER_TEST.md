# Student usability study

Status: protocol only. No participants, feedback, or measured impact yet.

The question is whether this interface helps a student distinguish an estimated trend from a conclusion that depends on assumptions. Three students or teachers can reveal confusing wording. Three people cannot establish a general educational effect.

Use anonymous labels such as P1, P2, and P3. Do not collect age, name, school, face, or voice. Participation is voluntary. A short written observation is sufficient; recording is unnecessary.

## Task

Give each person the app without an explanation of the calculations. Ask them to investigate Tehran temperature over 1981–2025, then explain their conclusion in two sentences. Next ask them to change the start year to 1986 and describe what changed. Finally, open the method evaluation and ask them what the Toronto comparison means.

Do not coach during the task. Record whether they can find the controls, state a rate with units and years, distinguish conditional evidence from proof, and recognize a dataset disagreement. If wording causes a mistake, record the actual mistake and revise the interface before repeating the task with another person.

## Fixed comprehension prompts

1. What does +0.50 °C per decade mean? It is an estimated rate across the specified historical period, not a forecast of every coming decade.
2. Does an interval that excludes zero prove the dataset is correct? No. The interval depends on assumptions and does not measure source bias.
3. Are four methods four independent confirmations? No. They use the same observations.
4. Does choosing a different time window necessarily reveal a software bug? No. The period and the average rate being estimated have changed.
5. Does the Toronto difference prove either dataset is wrong? No. The difference needs investigation with spatial support and processing differences considered.

The sentences after each prompt are scoring guidance for the facilitator. Do not show them before the person answers. Score each as correct, partly correct, or incorrect and keep the explanation, not just a number.
