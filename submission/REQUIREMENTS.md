## Two material eligibility concerns

The student chose the name and climate direction, but the record does not establish independent student authorship of the detailed solution idea. Disclose this accurately and ask the organizer how the rule applies. Do not imply that AI only completed routine code.

## Challenge objectives

The full [production brief](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/earth-system-trend-detective/) requires variable selection, regional data acquisition, a time series long enough for analysis, and a rate with significance assessment. The current implementation covers these elements through NASA POWER temperature and rain, coordinate selection, complete annual records, plots, slopes and conditional intervals. The brief accepts basic linear analysis; a neural model, automated variable discovery or additional complex tests are not mandatory.

The audience guidance and demonstrations are optional suggestions. Definitions, frozen examples and the proposed student task support that direction. POWER is not named in the resource list, but MERRA-2 is discussed in the brief and provides the retained POWER meteorology. The guide requires meaningful NASA resource use, not exclusive use of a particular listed portal.

The resources link [KNMI Climate Explorer](https://climexp.knmi.nl/), an existing scientific analysis service with the same name. Keep the student's chosen name, but identify this as an independent student prototype. Do not claim the name, plotting or significance testing are original. Our distinctiveness claim concerns the specific student workflow and its evidence; its educational value still needs testing.

## Judging criteria

The official [judging guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/) uses five criteria, each scored from 1 to 5. It supplies no probability of winning and no project-specific ranking.

| Criterion | Strongest evidence to show | Current weakness |

| Impact | A concrete student investigation and an actual usability result | Educational impact has not been measured |
| Creativity | Method and period comparisons, source disagreement, and replay in one understandable workflow | Scientific methods are established; broad originality is unproven |
| Validity | Numerical tests, synthetic failure cases, source checks, explicit assumptions | Some uncertainty methods fail in correlated short records; Toronto mismatch remains under investigation |
| Relevance | NASA data drive the calculation; all four challenge objectives have implementation evidence | Eligible entry and student ownership of the solution approach remain unresolved |
| Presentation | One question, visible workflow, one important limitation, evidence links | Student explanation and final demo need rehearsal |

The best fit appears to be the science or data award themes. That is a strategy judgment, not an official nomination or forecast of an award. Space Apps has multiple global award categories, rather than a single overall first-place prize.
