# Explaining Climate Explorer in your own words

Spend about forty minutes on these tasks. Keep the app open. You do not need to memorize every library function. You do need to know what a displayed number means and where it came from.

## First fifteen minutes

Open Tehran temperature for 1981-2025. Read the variable, period, source, units and rate. Explain the chart to someone who has not seen it.

Try these questions without reading an answer:

1. What does about 0.504 °C per decade mean for this selected historical period?
2. Is the fitted line a prediction for the next decade?
3. Does the city label mean NASA measured the temperature on a Tehran street?
4. Why are monthly temperatures weighted by calendar days?
5. What should happen if a month is missing?

Check your answers in EXPLANATION.md. The rate summarizes the selected record. It does not predict future weather or prove a cause. The data represent a model grid. A missing month excludes that year from this project's annual calculation.

## Next ten minutes

Inspect the ordinary interval and Newey-West comparisons. Explain why the point estimate stays the same while the interval can change. Then compare the date windows. Say which period each number describes.

An interval excluding zero is conditional evidence under its model. It is not a 95% probability that the claim is true. Agreement between methods on the same data is not independent confirmation. If you are unsure about a sentence, say so and trace the calculation rather than inventing an explanation.

## Next ten minutes

Open the evaluation evidence. Explain the no-trend simulation with strongly related yearly errors. It demonstrates a failure under a known synthetic scenario. It does not measure the probability that Tehran's result is wrong.

Then inspect Toronto. The source and nearby-location rates differ. The diagnostic narrows the investigation but does not prove a physical explanation. Explain why hiding this disagreement would weaken the investigation.

## Final five minutes

Download a result. Point to the raw source, source date and method settings. If Python is available, run the replay command. Explain what a matching hash does and does not prove.

Finish with a truthful contribution statement. For example: “I chose the project direction. Codex helped design and write the implementation. I reviewed these calculations and completed these tests.” Replace the final sentence with work you actually performed. Add dated evidence to the contribution log. Do not claim code or design work that you did not do.

## Questions to practice aloud

- What useful decision can a student make after inspecting the comparisons?
- What is the strongest evidence that the calculation works?
- What important result remains uncertain?
- Why did the project use an interpretable historical rate instead of a forecasting model?
- What would you change after observing a real student's mistakes?

If age-related media restrictions apply, practice privately. Do not put your voice, image or likeness into the public competition demonstration. The prepared fictional adult narrator needs no personal recording.

## Explain the engineering in version 1.3

Load the Tehran rainfall atlas. Choose an interior date window, open its evidence, and check that your worksheet notes remain. Download the full atlas and the selected investigation. Explain why one retains the full search while the other retains one exact result.

Practice these questions:

- Why are there 666 windows in this particular record?
- Why does the file keep the raw NASA response once?
- What does a filter change, and what does it leave unchanged?
- Why are overlapping windows not 666 independent tests?
- What do the browser, selected-record Python replay and complete-atlas verifier each check?
- What happens if a build fails or an old request reports an error after a newer one succeeds?

Use INNOVATION_REVIEW.md to explain one rejected alternative and the evidence needed to revisit it. Do not claim that every listed idea was built, validated or independently developed by you. Record your own actual calculation, test or code contribution with its date and evidence.
