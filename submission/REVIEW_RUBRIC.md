# Climate Explorer review rubric

Use this for a practical peer review. It is not a NASA score, award prediction, or substitute for eligibility review. The categories follow the current official judging guide, but the checks below are specific to this project. Give no combined score or invented weighting.

| Category | What to inspect | Evidence that would strengthen the project |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | Can a student complete a clear investigation task? Which explanation causes mistakes? | Observed task results from real users, including failures and revisions |
| Creativity | Does the workflow add value beyond an existing climate chart? | A clear comparison with established tools and a demonstrated benefit of the combined checks |
| Validity | Are data, units, intervals and limitations correct? Can an export be replayed? | Numerical references, source diagnostics, meaningful tests and reproducible results |
| Relevance | Do NASA data answer the chosen challenge's four core objectives? | Working variable selection, regional records, time plots, rates and conditional significance analysis |
| Presentation | Can someone understand the problem and proposed solution quickly? | A legible demo, coherent explanation, accessible links and honest claims |

For each category, record **not tested**, **needs work**, **clear**, or **strong evidence**. Use **not tested** when you lack evidence. These labels describe your review, not official judging results.

## Review record

For version 1.3, inspect these concrete changes before assigning a label:

- Load one of the current complete 1981-2025 atlases, choose a window, and follow its exact dates and source into the main analysis. Check that this case retains all 666 eligible candidate periods. Other date ranges or incomplete records can have different counts; use the payload's declared universe and verify it.
- Apply a direction filter. Confirm that it changes only the display and does not become a claim about the probability of a real trend.
- Verify a downloaded complete atlas and a selected investigation separately. Explain why these are different verification scopes.
- Watch the technical film and compare one displayed result with its retained JSON. The long film is portfolio media; the 29.8-second candidate remains separate. The real app image is release 1.1 and the atlas plate is a labeled data rendering, not a recorded app interaction.
- Listen to the actual final narration if reviewing sound quality. Automatic transcript agreement and visual review are recorded; human listening has not been performed by the development agents. Record your own listening outcome rather than inheriting a pass.

The 100-candidate review documents a design search and reasons for selection. It is not evidence of originality, measured usefulness or a hundred completed features. The missing human-task evidence in the Impact row remains missing until someone actually performs and records it.

## Stop conditions

A wrong calculation, inaccessible final-project link, mislabelled source, missing AI disclosure, or misleading authorship statement needs correction before sharing. An unresolved organizer decision must remain visible. A reviewer should not mark these resolved because the interface looks polished.

Source: [2026 Judging and Awards Guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/), checked on 13 November 2026. Each official criterion uses a 1-to-5 scale. This worksheet deliberately records observations instead of estimating an official score.
