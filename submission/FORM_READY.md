## 1. Project name

Climate Explorer

## 2. High-level project summary

Climate Explorer helps students question historical temperature and rainfall claims using NASA data. It compares fitted rates across dates and statistical assumptions, then saves the source and calculations for another person to check. Its complete date-window atlas makes selective choices visible. The proposed benefit is a more careful, repeatable investigation. Learning benefit has not yet been measured.

## 5. Detailed project description

A climate headline can change when its author chooses different dates. In the same retained NASA POWER record, Tehran rainfall has a fitted rate of +36.9 mm/year per decade over 1981-2025 and -52.8 over 2011-2025. The shorter ordinary interval includes zero. Opposite signs do not establish a permanent reversal. Climate Explorer lets a reader inspect the choices behind that result.

The public application offers temperature and precipitation for five locations across four fixed periods ending in 2025. These forty analyses use retained NASA responses and make no live NASA request. The Python application supports live queries for custom coordinates and periods with at least ten complete years. It forms annual temperature means using calendar-day weights. It multiplies monthly precipitation rates by calendar days and sums the totals. Incomplete years are excluded. POWER represents a model grid, not a local weather station or a street measurement.

The investigation shows annual observations, a monthly view and an ordinary linear rate. It compares Newey-West intervals at recorded lags, a Theil-Sen slope, fixed period checks and the effect of leaving out each year. These checks share data and are not independent confirmations. Ordinary intervals assume independent, approximately normal errors with constant variance. Adjusted intervals can still perform poorly in short correlated records.

The atlas enumerates all contiguous periods of at least ten calendar years within each retained record. A fit also needs ten complete annual observations. Each complete 45-year case has 666 windows, giving 6,660 across ten cases. Selecting a cell opens its exact source and period. Filters only change the display. Overlapping windows estimate different periods. Their signs are not votes or direction probabilities, and browsing all windows does not correct significance for selection. Ten years is an exploration boundary, not proof of reliable climate detection.

Eight method configurations were compared on 20,000 synthetic records with known generating trends. No deployable candidate passed every internal screening case. A five-location temperature comparison with NASA GISTEMP retains a substantial Toronto disagreement. That comparison has different spatial supports and is not station or precipitation validation. These failures and limits remain visible.

Exports retain the raw NASA response, request, retrieval time, units, settings, software versions and hashes. Browser checks recalculate aggregation and point results. Python replay also recalculates the selected uncertainty results and checks context. Complete-atlas verification additionally checks the whole window universe. A hash checks consistency, not source truth or human authorship.

The proposed audience is students and teachers. No educational effect or user endorsement has been established. This is historical exploration, not forecasting or causal attribution. Climate plotting, statistical methods and start/end trend matrices already exist. The proposed contribution is their combination with selected-period reconstruction and repeatable evidence. This independent prototype is separate from KNMI Climate Explorer and has no NASA affiliation or endorsement.

## 7. NASA data

NASA POWER supplies the main monthly T2M and PRECTOTCORR records for 1981-2025 in the retained examples. T2M is mean air temperature at two metres in degrees Celsius. PRECTOTCORR is corrected precipitation, supplied as a monthly mean rate in mm/day. Calendar-day weighting produces annual mean temperature; rate multiplication and summation produce approximate annual rainfall totals because the source rates are rounded. Retained responses identify MERRA-2 as their meteorology source. Its native grid is 0.5 degrees latitude by 0.625 degrees longitude. Source URLs, retrieval times, API versions, units and payload hashes travel with the evidence.

NASA POWER data are provided by the NASA POWER Project at NASA Langley Research Center. Service: https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/monthly/. Meteorology methodology: https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/methodology/data/sources_meteorology/. Acknowledgment guidance: https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/acknowledgements/.

GISTEMP Team, 2026: GISS Surface Temperature Analysis, version 4. NASA Goddard Institute for Space Studies. Accessed 11 November 2026. https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/. The comparison uses the land-only 250 km station-smoothing NetCDF at https://data.giss.nasa.gov/pub/gistemp/gistemp250_GHCNv4.nc.gz, with matched complete years and explicit spatial differences. Download description: https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/data_v4.html. Lenssen et al. (2024), A GISTEMPv4 observational uncertainty ensemble, Journal of Geophysical Research: Atmospheres, 129, e2023JD040179. https://doi.org/10.1029/2023JD040179.

The five POWER responses were retained on 11 November 2026. Their exact requests, annual values and hashes, together with the frozen GISTEMP file history and hash, are recorded at https://github.com/ArmanDamirchilou/climate-explorer/blob/main/evaluation/DATASET_CHECK.md and in its linked JSON and source snapshots. Later requests can return revised data. The comparison does not establish either dataset as ground truth, resolve Toronto's disagreement, or validate precipitation.
